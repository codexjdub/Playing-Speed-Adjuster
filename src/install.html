Try it

Start a track, then click your PSA bookmark, or the PSA button above. Each track beeps once a second, so at 2× you hear two beeps a second, at the same pitch. With both tracks here, ‹ › in the panel switches between them.

Install

  1. Show your bookmarks bar: ⌘ Shift B on a Mac, Ctrl Shift B on Windows and Linux.
  2. Drag the PSA button above onto the bar.
  3. On any page with audio or video, click PSA. Click it again, or ×, to close the panel.

Firefox: the extension

In Firefox you can add PSA from Firefox Add-ons instead. It runs on every page without a click: your speed is applied as soon as something plays, a small pill shows it, and the toolbar button opens the full panel. It updates itself, and its settings page has a speed for sites without one of their own. It needs Firefox 140 or later, on computers and on Android.

Can't drag it?

Create a bookmark named PSA and paste the code as its address:

  • Chrome, Edge: right-click the bookmarks bar, choose Add page…
  • Firefox: right-click the bookmarks toolbar, choose Add Bookmark…
  • Safari: bookmark any page with ⌘ D, then right-click the bookmark and choose Edit Address…
  • Phones and tablets: bookmark this page, then edit the bookmark and replace its address. On Android Chrome, run it by typing “PSA” in the address bar and tapping the bookmark.

What it does

Any speed

0.25× to 4×: presets, ± steps, a typed value like 1.6, or the keys [ ] and \ for 1.5×.

Stays put

Remembers your speed on each site and puts it back when the site tries to reset it. It also remembers where you left off in long recordings.

Every player

Works with several players on one page, including ones added later. ‹ › switches between them and outlines the one you picked.

Knows what's playing

Shows a title, Play/Pause, −10 s / +20 s skips and the real time left at your speed for the item that most recently started. Videos get a picture-in-picture button.

Out of the way

Drag the panel anywhere, or shrink it to a small pill with − and + and a menu of speeds. It stays on top, even over fullscreen video and the page's pop-ups, until you close it.

Private

Makes no network requests, and the bookmarklet runs only when you click it. Settings and positions stay in your browser.

Good to know

  • Media inside another site's embedded player, such as a YouTube video embedded in a blog, can't be reached by the bookmarklet. Open the video's own page and use PSA there, or use the Firefox extension, which applies your speed inside embedded players too.
  • Keyboard shortcuts while the panel is open, even minimized: [ slows down by 0.1, ] speeds up by 0.1, \ sets 1.5×. They're ignored while you type in a text field, and ⌘ or Ctrl combinations are left to the browser.
  • Live streams, such as live radio, stay at normal speed: they can't run ahead of the broadcast, so speeding them up would only make them buffer. The panel shows “Live · normal speed”.
  • The speed is saved per site in that site's local storage, so clearing the site's data resets it.
  • Where you left off is saved for recordings longer than 3 minutes, but only while the panel is open. Play one again from the start and the panel offers Resume at ….
  • The picture-in-picture button only appears where the browser lets web pages start picture-in-picture. Elsewhere, use the browser's own picture-in-picture button.
  • Works in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari. Every change is tested automatically in all three browser engines (Chromium, Firefox and WebKit); please report problems on GitHub.
